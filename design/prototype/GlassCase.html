Problem

Wealth advisors answer client questions under suitability rules. A wrong answer is a compliance problem, and a confident wrong answer is worse. A general chat assistant can’t show where an answer came from, and it rarely says “I don’t know”.

GlassBox answers only from a client’s approved documents (the investment policy statement, fund factsheets and regulation) and refuses everything else. A compliance seat reviews flagged answers under a four-eyes rule.

Constraints

Cite approved documents only. Anything else is refused, with the missing source named.
Replayable. Every decision can be replayed later, by someone else.
Multi-tenant. One firm’s data and audit chain never touch another’s.
Small and cheap. Runs on one small instance for about $14 a month.

Architecture

Every path ends in the audit log. A claim the scorers can’t ground turns the answer into a refusal.

What broke, and the limits

  • The benchmark is author-written, so it can’t measure what I didn’t think to test.
  • The scorers learned from small, synthetic or bootstrapped sets. Reviewer labels are now exported so they can be retrained on real judgments.
  • The hash chain is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: the chain head isn’t anchored outside the system yet.
