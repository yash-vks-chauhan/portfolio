01

Problem

Wealth advisors answer client questions under suitability rules. A wrong answer is a compliance problem, and a confident wrong answer is worse. A general chat assistant can’t show where an answer came from, and it rarely says “I don’t know”.

GlassBox answers only from a client’s approved documents (the investment policy statement, fund factsheets and regulation) and refuses everything else. A compliance seat reviews flagged answers under a four-eyes rule.

02

Constraints

  • C1Cite approved documents only. Anything else is refused, with the missing source named.
  • C2Every decision must be replayable later, by someone else.
  • C3Multi-tenant: one firm’s data and audit chain never touch another’s.
  • C4Run on one small instance for about $14 a month.

03

Architecture

Every path ends in the audit log. A claim the scorers can’t ground turns the answer into a refusal.

04

Decisions

AcceptedDecision 1

Refuse instead of guessing

Chose
Out-of-scope questions are refused. The refusal names the missing source and offers one-click escalation to compliance.
Over
[The alternative you weighed, e.g. answering with a disclaimer]
Because
An advisor can act on a refusal. They can’t check a guess.
Cost
Lower coverage, and more escalations for compliance to handle.
AcceptedDecision 2

Small scikit-learn scorers, not an LLM judge

Chose
Three small models: a grounding scorer (logistic regression on embedding similarity plus numeric match), a refusal router and a fallback classifier.
Over
[An LLM-as-judge, or another option you tried]
Because
Deterministic and fast: determinism 1.00 and p95 latency 1 ms on the benchmark.
Cost
Small training sets (320 / 240 / 160 rows), synthetic or bootstrapped.
AcceptedDecision 3

Hash-chain the log per tenant; forbid DELETE

Chose
Each decision is stored with its passages, kept and dropped claims and trust scores, and chained per tenant. The database refuses DELETE on audit tables.
Over
[Application-level logging only, or the option you weighed]
Because
Tampering becomes detectable, and any answer can be replayed.
Cost
Tamper-evident, not tamper-proof, until the chain head is anchored outside the system.

07

What broke, and the limits

  • —The benchmark is author-written, so it can’t measure what I didn’t think to test.
  • —The scorers learned from small, synthetic or bootstrapped sets. Reviewer labels are now exported so they can be retrained on real judgments.
  • —The hash chain is tamper-evident, not tamper-proof: the chain head isn’t anchored outside the system yet.
