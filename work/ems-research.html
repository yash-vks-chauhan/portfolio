In short

At IIT Madras I worked on Tamil Nadu’s 108 ambulance service: a pipeline that separates real operational drift from context a district can’t control, then checks who bears the burden.1

Ambulance analytics for Tamil Nadu: real drift, separated from context a district can’t control, then an equity check.1

Problem

An ambulance service’s times drift for many reasons. Some are operational and fixable. Others, like metro congestion or tribal terrain, are context a district can’t control, and they fail in different ways.2

Treat them the same and you can’t tell what to fix. This work separates the two, then measures who bears the burden.

Ambulance times drift for many reasons: some operational and fixable, some (metro congestion, tribal terrain) context a district can’t control. This work separates the two, then checks who bears the burden.2

Data

Five-plus years. Dispatch and road-construction data, with 100+ data-quality checks.
Five service legs. Response, on-scene, travel, hospital handover and return, analysed separately.
Government data. Only aggregate results are shown here.

MethodHow the analysis runs

Each stage answers one question: is this leg unusual, by how much, and who carries it.

  1. 1Split each call into five service legs
  2. 2Embed each stage with a graph neural network
  3. 3Flag anomalies with a VAE
  4. 4Size each anomaly with counterfactual XGBoost
  5. Compare who bears the burden, with a Composite Burden Index

Findings

Aggregate findings from the manuscript, which isn’t published yet.2

  • 1Neonatal and paediatric cases bear the highest burden on the response and return legs.
  • 2Behavioural cases bear it at hospital handover.
  • 3Gender parity holds on four of five legs; the exception is on-scene time for female patients.
  • 4Metro congestion and tribal terrain are different failure paths.
  • 5No evidence of systemic neglect of disadvantaged socio-economic groups.

My part

Third of three authors. I ingested five-plus years of dispatch and road-construction data with 100+ data-quality checks, built the GraphSAGE → VAE → XGBoost workflow for drift and anomaly analysis, and ran the equity analysis across metro, tribal and rural districts.1
